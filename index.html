The Viswanath Lab connects glioma metabolism to two linked outputs: therapies that exploit a metabolic dependency, and imaging biomarkers that show whether a drug reached the brain and changed tumor biology. That bridge runs from patient-derived cells and isotope tracing to orthotopic models, longitudinal imaging, pharmacokinetics, and survival.

I built a small review tool over 77 raw 1H-MRS acquisitions from a UCSF mutant-IDH inhibitor study, then connected the scan-level evidence to the paper's cross-model 2-HG, glutamate, and GLX response tables. It asks two practical questions: which spectra are ready for fitting, and which biological signal is robust enough to advance into the next cell/organoid and in vivo validation study?

How it works

The Dryad deposit contains 308 files organized as 77 Varian PRESS acquisitions: one FID, acquisition log, parameter file, and text record per scan. I parsed the raw complex signal, verified protocol and completion fields, generated a review-only magnitude spectrum, and estimated relative drift by cross-correlating each spectrum to the cohort median. The acquisition comments expose treatment labels and study days, but not a documented model identity for every file, so this explorer does not invent that mapping.

The second analysis uses the reported early concentration means from three tumor models. It recalculates percent change versus the matched control and applies one transparent gate: 2-HG must fall while Glu and GLX rise by at least the selected threshold. Changing that threshold changes the advance/hold recommendation and the proposed next experiment.

Why this matters for the lab

The applied Specialist role spans the exact handoff this demo makes: drug-response and mechanistic assays in patient-derived glioma systems, isotope tracing of metabolic adaptation, orthotopic dosing and longitudinal imaging, and PK/brain-penetration assessment. A raw-scan review queue helps protect the imaging endpoint; a threshold-sensitive response gate helps decide what still needs to be resolved before a combination or biomarker is treated as cross-model.

What I would bring

I have worked with mouse glioblastoma models, IVIS imaging, tissue collection, pharmacokinetic and safety analyses, mammalian cell culture, qPCR, flow cytometry, and computational biology. Here I used those two sides together: code to audit the public imaging data, and experimental reasoning to connect an early metabolic signal to the controls and readouts needed for cell/organoid, isotope-tracing, orthotopic, IVIS, PK, and survival validation.

Methods note

The raw-data analysis uses the CC BY 4.0 Dryad deposit for Radoul et al.. All 77 acquisitions contain 10,000 complex points, 512 averages, TE 20 ms, and TR 4 s, and all logs end in “Acquisition complete.” The spectrum is shown on a relative frequency axis; no peak is labeled as a metabolite and no concentration is inferred from it. The paper's reported biological group sizes were U87IDHmut control/AG-881/BAY n = 4/4/5, BT257 n = 10/6/5, and SF10417 n = 6/6/5. The public file comments do not document a scan-to-model map, so scan-level labels are limited to treatment and day. No verified Viswanath Lab or PI-owned GitHub repository was found after checking official pages, publication statements, web GitHub results, and repository search; the 2025 Cell Reports paper also states that it reports no original code. This is an exploratory QC and decision aid, not evidence that any new treatment result has been experimentally validated.
